### Electromotive Force
Denoted as $\varepsilon$, it is the work done per unit charge by a source.
This is the "force" experienced by a unit charge in a circuit, where:
- Electric field $\vec{E}$ points from high to low potential,
- Source field $\vec{f}_s$ points from low to high potential in the source region, and
- $\vec{f}_s = -\vec{E}$ for ideal sources.
This can be related to potential difference through
$$
\varepsilon = \int_a^b \vec{f}_s \cdot d\vec{l} = -\int_a^b\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l}
$$
such that
$$
\varepsilon = V(b)-V(a) = \Delta V
$$
##### Motional electromotive force
A moving wire through a magnetic field. We follow the Lorentz force law:
$$
|\vec{f}_\text{mag}| = vB
$$
such that
$$
\varepsilon = \oint \vec{f}_\text{mag}\cdot d\vec{l} = vBh
$$
Essentially, we get a magnetic battery.

NOTE: It is not the $\vec{B}$ field that is doing the work. Remember that we still have the wire motion---the energy comes from the force that makes this wire move.
