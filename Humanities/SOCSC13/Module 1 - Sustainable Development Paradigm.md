### UN Conference on Human Environment (1972)
- Limits to Growth foretold sharp decline in economic, social, and environmental conditions by 21st century
- Should the pursuit of growth-centric development continue to ignore costs to environment and drive social exclusion, human welfare decline will be unavoidable
##### Neoclassical Growth Theory
- Assumption: All human beings act rationally -> flat characterization
- For economics: well-being = welfare
	- Focus: wealth-creation
	- Create wealth, then distribute
- Two models:
	- Business as usual: assumes depletion of non-renewables limits production, living standards stop rising w/ industrial growth
	- Comprehensive technology scenario: assumes high tech development and adoption, reduced pollution, resource efficiency improvements
	- Business as usual 2: demonstrates climate tipping point; resource depletion will not avoid collapse, pollution crisis will be cause
	- Stable World: assumes CT + changes in societal values and priorities; stabilized population, high level of human welfare

### Tragedy of the Commons
- Imagine an open pasture shared by multiple cattle owners. Herd of each owner is increased to maximize one's benefit
- With an unregulated resource, this is "logical" since the benefit enjoyed by the individual and the impact is shared by all.
	- This, however, leads to ultimate overgrazing of the pasture.
##### The remorseless workings of things / Free Riding in the commons
- in the classic narrative of a complex ecosystem offering a shared resources (commons) subject to social dilemmas:
	- highly valued and limited resource
	- 2 or more actors benefitting from consumption
	- individual withdrawals
- Self-interest action tends to spread out its negative effects across a larger population
- Optimizing for oneself in the short term isn't optimizing for anyone in the long-term
- Resource regulation solutions
	- Government intervention and privatization
	- Self-organizing by those directly involved (the resource appropriators)
- Examples of commons: ocean fisheries, irrigation systems, traffic jams, bank run on scarce goods, labor union organizing, common pool resources, public goods and bads, institutions requiring long-term trust building
- "Even if I change my ways, others will not"
	- Addressing pollution / environmental degradation is costly in short-term, a disincentive and encountering resistance
- Free riding is incentivized
	- Those failing to change individual behavior can still stand to benefit from difficult changes made by others
	- Widespread free riding results in the Tragedy of Commons, everyone needs to act to counteract enviromental decline, but no one has an incentive to implement them individually
