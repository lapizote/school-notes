##### The Monetary Approach
- Identifies poverty with a shortfall in consumption (income) from some poverty line
- Based on:
	- Valuation of different components of income or consumption at market prices
	- Imputation of equivalent values for production activities/items not valued through the market e.g. housework, unpaid childcare and care of the elderly at home
	- commonly used based on the assumption of being an appropriate proxy of welfare and poverty
- Montary indicators represent a convenient shortcut method to identify those who are poor, not only in terms of lack of resources but also of outcomes (nutrition, education, health)
- Purchasing power is a measure of human welfare
- Origins: 1800s-1900s, Charles Boothe and Seebohm Rowntree (London & York)
- Interested in: Which households are "living in obvious want and squalor?"
- Welfarist View: heterogenous factors can be controlled using objective data sources, but which disfavor those who have less purchasing power
- Seebohm Rowntree coined "poverty line"
	- a minimum threshold of income for not being poor; measure of absolute poverty (standards across countries) or of relative poverty (relative to society's own benchmarks)
	- Basis: cost of basic needs; nutritionally based information (Food Energy Intake Method)
	- Assertion: If it's monetary, it's scientific!

So how effective is money as a proxy for measuring deprivations in the many dimensions of poverty? Caveats:
- Poverty is not exclusively an economic category
- Economic theory is insufficient, no theory of poverty that differentiates poor from non-poor
- Objective assessments and value judgments are externally determined by experts based on consumption baskets
- Mostly individual achievements are addressed. How about society-wide social phenomena?

Traditional monetary-based measures: GDP and GNI
- Gross Domestic Product per Capita: Total final output of goods and services produced by country's economy, within the country's territory (by residents and non-residents, regardless of its allocation between domestic and foreign claims) over a given time period divided by the total country population
	- Measures the flow of new output, an indicator of living standards
	- Not included in the PH context: remittances from OFWs
	- Why is it useful: can aggregate over time
- Gross National Income: total domestic and foreign output claimed by citizens of a country
### Freedom-based Capabilities Approach
- Capabilities (A. Sen)
	- Ability of an individual to satisfy certain crucially important functionings up to a certain minimally adequate levels
	- Defines well-being as the freedom of an individual to live a valued life
	- Functionings = valued achievements
- Rejects the assumption of utility maximization in order to achieve a state of well-bein
- Utility as "desire fulfillment" is insufficient
- Difference between:
	- famine and fasting
	- hunger experience by destitute person today and in a month
	- changing residence from one place to another and forced displacement
- Outcomes characterize the quality of life
	- Rather than income, evaluation of well-being focuses on non-monetary indicators
	- Differences in metabolic rates, physical ability, etc.
- To obtain the same valuable achievements, some people need more resources than others
	- Individuals need diff amounts of commodities to have good health

#### Five Instrumental Freedoms
- Political freedoms: liberties to determine who should govern and on what principles
- Economic facilities: occasions to utilize economic resources for consumption
- Social opportunites: arrangements for public health and education access to overcome deprivations
- Transparency guarantees: necessity for disclosure, clarity and openness in civil/social interactions
- Protective securities: social safety nets, unemployment benefits and insurance

##### Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)
- Human development conceptually underpinned by CA
- CA translated into Human Poverty Index, deprivation in 3 essential elements of human life:
	- longevity
	- knowledge
	- decent standards of living
	- these are indicators designed for developing countries
- Evolution in 2010: MPI - people are multidimensionally poor if they are deprived in 1/3 or 10 weighted indicators
### Participatory Approach
##### Hidden Dimensions of Poverty
- Process of Merging Knowledge, combining 3 types:
	- knowledge gained from life experience of those trapped in poverty
	- knowledge gained by professionals to serve most deprived people (teachers, healthcare workers,)
	- academic knowledge, indispensable but partial
- Core Experience of Poverty
	- Places anguish and agency at center of conceptualization poverty:
		- Disempowerment
		- Suffering in body, mind, heart
		- Struggle and resistance
- Relational Dynamics of Poverty
	- Everyday relationships between individuals, social groups, institutions shape poverty experience:
		- Institutional maltreatment
		- Social maltreatment
		- Unrecognized contributions
- Points of Critique
	- Selectivity bias in decision-making
		- Tokenism
	- Condoning or reinforcing existing social relations
		- Whose voices are being heard? Opposition/dissent represented, or fearfulness prevails?
	- Bearing of social conditioning
		- Bias in self-assessment/self-rating of poverty
		- Objective conditions of vulnerable populations can be overlooked
