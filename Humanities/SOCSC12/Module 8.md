### The Swirl
- The murmuration of starlings
- Starlings tend to have a mass death, exposure to chemicals
- The Monsanto Company holds the patent for Roundup, a strong herbicide containing glyphosate, used mainly as weed control
- Glyphosate is used in agricultural and non-agriculutrl setting (especially for lawn management)
	- Used in four major cash crops: rapeseed, corn, soybeans, and sugar beets
	- The majority of products in the US most likely contain glyphosate and can be associate with numerous health hazards
- Monsanto is one prime example of companies that sponsor research to justify the "safety" of their agrochemicals
	- Non-consensus creates what Adams describes as "The Swirl"
	- Academic Capitalism
		- Even the "truth" created by science is manipulated by capital
- "Glyphosate offers a bird's eye view of how chemicals that may or may not be harmful are able to simultaneously inhabit contradictory truth statements... Glyphosate thus weaves together an interesting swirl of normaive science, providing us with a great example of how cheemicals can disrupt not just cells, microbes, and genes, but also scientific efforts to establish facts by persistently arbitrating them and refusing to let them be."
- "Forms of life are not harmed in the same way by glyphosate as it moves through bodies and permeates different tissues at different rates in different species, and the question of just how harmful it is to bodies remains contested despite there being concrete eidence of this possibility."
- "The swirl is more than a metaphor of knowledge---it is a model for capturing the movement of glyphosate in its penetrations and shaping of the worlds it inhabits. To be even more concrete about this idea, one might consider the impact glyphosate is probably having on birds like starlings and on their patterns of murmuration."
	- The swirl is everywhere. It permeates all.
	- Glyphosate can be an activist, because it exposes itself to be visible.
- In the Philippines:
	- The government normalizes the air pollution, a narrative akin to the swirl
- We have a limited perspective given to us by The Swirl
### Trust without Confidence: Moving Medicine w/ Dirty Hands (Kali Rubaii)
Dilemma: "She knows that she will never connect with some people politically that the medicine passes through, but it needs to pass through those people for the medicine to arrive at the people who need it."
- Two options:
	- No longer give medicine to rid herself of the dirtiness of politics
	- Give the medicine, but to trust the ones who have dirty hands.
- You have to navigate around the maze of politics and real life, but still stick with your principles.
	- The Swirl: Truth can be subjective and manipulated.
	- This has to something that all must learn: How does one maintain themselves through the politics of those more powerful but to remain one's humanity?
	- How can you see beyond the Swirl of reality, so you can see truthfully what the reality is?
### Narratives from the Rice Fields (Jehu Laniog)
##### Nabua, Camarines Sur
- Land area of 37.14 sq mi
- In between Bula Lake and Lake Bato
##### Negotiations:
- Synthetic Fertilizers
- Flooding
- Seedlings
- Soil Acidity
##### Problem:
- The agricultural technologies developed through the project of the green revolution (1960s) continue to erode solidarity built by multiple agricultural crisis
- The technologies are not fit for the contexts of the farmers; their problems are being individualized
- The most oppressed people in the Philippines: farmers
- The problem with hybrid seeds: more output, but also more input
	- Farmers can only use the seeds once
- Rice seedling distribution: Farmers' context are not considered, seedlings given blindly
- Treated with chemicals: Not for human or animal consumption
##### Argument:
- New agricultural technologies and government programs shoud take into account diverse cultures, agricultural practices, agricultural decision-making, and the nexus of colonial legacies in the hometowns to enable the promise of self-sufficiency.
