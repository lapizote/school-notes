### "Islamic Art?"
- Risks being reductive---collapses cultural diversity of multiple civilisations into a single term
- Attempts to treat "Islamic" as cultural rather than purely religious
	- Focuses on regional and temporal specificities
- "Indo-Persian" axis and maritime exclusion---tendency to highlight Umayyad, Seljuks, Ottomans, Mughals
	- privileges artistic production of Persianite and Arabic lands
- Challenge the narative of SEA
- Trasmission of Islam across SEA was accommodative rather than coercive
	- peaceful spread via maritime networks, merchants, Sufi mystics
	- \
- Cross-contact across communities in the Islamic World
### Seeing is Believing
- There is an importance in seeing in how they see things in a faith-based perspective
- Tawhid tenet: Oneness of Allah---the sole creator
	- As such, the goal of art is not mimesis (imitation) but to evoke divine unity
	- Art becomes vehicle for contemplating Allah's attributes
		- 99 names of Allah
	- Art as act of remembrance (dhikr) of our mortal-ness and contemplation (musyahadah)
	- Visual environment in mosques is to show an outer physical beauty and inner spiritual beauty
### Before Islam
- Important to consider what artistic traditions existed before Islam
- "What happens when a religion becomes the dominant faith?"
	- Is tradition broken? New form and style?
	- Or elements are incorporated into new art?
### Calligraphy & Inscriptions as Motifs
 - Visible marker for Islamicisation of a culture
	 - Requirement of arabic as part of Islamic tradition
 - Adoption and adaptation of Arabic script as main writing system for Malay (Jawi)
	 - Also applicable in other SEA languages (e.g. Javanese/pegon, Bugis/serang)
	 - Producing a copy of the Quran is a way to remember Allah
 - Development of regional sytles with regards to Quranic illumination
 - Illumination---decoration on pages of the Quran
	 - done by hand
	 - Often fills the whole page
	 - usually done by multiple people (scribe for script, artists for rest)
	 - use of yellow (symbol of royalty in SEA)
	 - Javanese: 
- Diacritic marks next to text not written in native language
	- Likely writer is not a native speaker
- Islamic art does not necessarily have to involve Arabic
	- Examples of tombstones with both Chinese and Jawi scripts
