### Care Work
- Labour oriented towards nurturing and sustaining other people
	- relational, embodied, emotional
- Encomasses:
	- Domestic work (childcare,  elderly care, cooking, cleaning)
	- Child and elderly care (outside homes)
	- Teaching
	- Nursing
	- Sex work
- Overlaps with labour in social reproduction
	- Differs in analytic scale
	- Social reproduction emphasizes structural processes by which capitalism reproduces workforce and social relations
	- Meanwhile, care work emphasizes relational and practical dynamics involved in caring for others
- Humans have always done care work, usually unpaid
	- Elites have paid for care work (or through slavery)
	- Growing numbers of paid care workers
	- Feminized labour in many contexts
- Not universally feminized labor
	- e.g. Batek in Malaysia
		- No exclusively male or female characteristics
		- Women and men can do all forms of labour
		- Childrearing a shared responsibility
### Putting the Work in Care Work
- Often not recognized as work
- Valorization of waged work outside of home under capitalism (esp. with neoliberalism)
	- But capitalism relies on unpaid care work!
	- When paid, low wages
	- Considered "low-skilled"
- Devaluation harms women (esp lower class, minoritized, and/or migrant)
- Makes it seem that women doing unpaid care work full-time don't contribute to society
- Makes it seem like it should be easy for women to do paid work and domestic labour - the "second shift"
### Migration
- Since 1970s, growing numbers of care workers are interational migrants
	- Hmmm, neoliberalism?
- Approx. 67.1 million domestic workers in the world, 11.5 million are international migrants (3/4 women)
- Typically, Global South to Global North
- 1/5th of Singaporean households (up 27% in a decade)
### Global Transfer of Care
- Overall, men have done little to increase contribution to domestic work
- Limited state support for social reproduction
- Economic inequalities and policies shape migration from Global South to Global North for domestic work
- Traditional division of labour between gender has metaphorically gone global---Global North being cared for by Global South
- Care is being extracted like resources during colonial era
### Domestic workers and sexuality
- Control over migrant women's bodies via employer and state policies
- Concerns over sexuality of migrant domestic workers
	- Might steal husband, might get pregnant, etc.
	- Domestic workers required to take STI and pregnancy tests
- Stereotypes based on class and nationality
- Constable's article
### Sex Work
- Encompasses a wide range of sexual services, wide range of working conditions (e.g. spaces, control, pay)
- Part of broader sex industry
	- Stipping, web-cams, etc.
- Workers come from wide variety of backgrounds
- Feminized labour (but also male and gender-nonconforming sex workers)
- Blurry boundaries to what counts as sex work
- Relationships that involve monetary
- Along with other forms of care work, sex work is relational, embodied, emotional
- Close physical and emotional contact w/ clients
- Involves emotional labour
	- Managing one's emotions in order to produce positive feelings in other people
	- Intimate - often in private spaces, involving few people`
### Stigma
- Double standards: permissible for men to go to sex workers while sex workers are stigmatized
- High rates of violence against sex workers
- Controls women's sexuality more broadly
### Legality
- Varying legal frameworks for sex work
- Either: completely illegal; sex workers criminalized; clientele criminalized; decriminalized; legalized and regulated
- Activists argue that legalization leads to better worker protections, but can be complicated
### Race
- Long histories rooted in colonialism shapes how race intersects w/ sexual representations and desires
- Particular racialized women portrayed as hypersexual
- e.g. Brazilian women, especialy mulatas
- Atlanta Shootings, March 16 2021
	- 8 people killed in massage parlors (6 were Asian women)
### Debates
- Sex work can involve coercion (trafficking; economic pressures)
- Are sex workers victims, or does this label strip them of agency?
- Does sex work uphold male domination, or can it be empowering (if voluntary)?
- Raises questions over issues of choice and empowerment
