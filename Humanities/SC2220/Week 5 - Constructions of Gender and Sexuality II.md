How can we categorize gender and sexual non-comformity?
### Gender and sexual non-conformity
- Across time and space, there are:
	- Gender identities and expressions that transgresses dominant feminine/masculine norms
	- Sexual desires and relationships that transgress dominant heterosexual norms
- Nonnormative, transgressive, gender-variant, gender/sexual minorities, queer?
	- Anthropologists say that these terms are complicated
### "Third Gender"
- First used (primarily in US and European scholarship) in the 1970s
- Applied to institutionalized gender categories and practices in some societies that aren't normatively male or female
- Scholars and activists think beyond gender binary
	- e.g. hijras in South Asia, bissu among Bugis people in Indonesia
- Bissu
	- embody both male and female characteristics
	- Embody both human and spirit characteristics
	- Part of creation stories
	- Ritual role in bestowing blessings
	- Historical role as court advisors
- Subsumes all non-Western non-conforming identities, practices, terminologies, and histories as "third", which can gloss over differences
- May imply that in contexts that its applied to there's only one alternative to binary gender categories
	- Not necessarily the case!
- Can imply that "first" and "second" categories are inviolable and stable
### Transgender
- Recent years, used to replace "third gender"
- Its cultural specificity means that if its applied too generally, it can gloss over differences
- Has a specific history in the US, with gay and lesbian advocates working to separate sexual orientation from gender identity
### Hijras
- People in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh assigned male at birth but engage in practices normatively associated with cisgender women
- Differing claims to definition:
	- Removal of male genitals
	- Asexual, or receptive sexual partner of a panti
	- Ritual role conferring fertility, or sex work
- Even the identity of the term itself is up in the air
- Often reduced to their gender identity
- Generally lower class
- Complex lived experiences in terms of religion, kinship, and other factors
- Some self-identified hijras who identify as trans
- There are also people in South Asia who are trans but not hijra
- In Bangladesh: legal recognition as a gender category in 2013, but only people with ambiguous genitals
### Lesbian
- Used to describe women who have sexual relationships with other women
- Used in European medical texts in late 19th century
- Takes on different meanings
### Bodily practice
- Mainstream global LGBTQ+ discourses (rooted in American and European understandings) center on the assigned sex of who someone has sex with
- In some contexts, person's role during sex is key to identity regarding sexuality and gender
- e.g. kotis and pantis
### Identity
- The relationship between sexual acts and sexual identities is not a fixed one, and it is projected from the observer's time and place to others at great peril
- Idea of a unitary, core, true character that's often implicit in
- Emic perspective: understanding the group being studied on their own terms
- Etic perspective: understanding the group being studied from the anthropologist's perpsective
- Anthropologists tack back and forth between these perspectives
- Wekker highlights importance of the emic
### Laws
- Array of legal rulings regarding sexuality and gender in terms of sex, marriage, employment, documentation, etc.
- Public sentiment varies widely
- Experiences of discrimination and violence
### Inclusivity
- In many places, laws and sentiments are becoming more inclusive
- Ex. non-binary gender recognition and legal documents in Iceland
- activists across the globe advocating for change
- Not just victims; vibrant communities
