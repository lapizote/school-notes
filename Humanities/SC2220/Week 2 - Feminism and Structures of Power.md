### What is Feminism?
- Dictionary definition: theory of political, economic, and social equality of the sexes
- bell hooks: a movement to end sexist oppression
	- 1984, context in the US: Good to have historical context and to find what still resonates today
	- Issues discussed still have bearing in variety of loacles
	- Negative perceptions of feminism
	- "Anything goes" understanding of feminism in pop culture -> according to hooks, this dilutes the meanings
		- Creates stances that reinforce forms of oppression
- A definition of focused on equality with men overlooks other social structures that produce domination and oppression
	- Even men are unequal with each other!
	- Inequalities independent of gender exists
- Reforming only helps privileged few
- Political commitment to end forms of domination can positively transform lives across social groups
- "A starting point indicating the direction of exploration and analysis" for feminist scholarship (p. 33 of bell hooks reading)
	- Feminist scholarship is inherently political
### Patriarchy
- A social formatioon of male-gendered power with a particular structure (Ortner p. 308)
	- Male hierarchy
	- Male solidarity
	- Female exclusion
	- Disdain for women and "wrong" men
- Found in many arenas of social life in many places
- Intersectional, and is connected to other structures of domination and oppression
- "Assume and produce a notion of manhood that is inseparable from racial superiority, heterosexuality, and able-bodiness... other major aspects of social superiority"
### Gender egalitarianism
- Patriarchy is widespread, but not universal
- In gender egalitarian contexts, women and men have similar opportunities, and neither are assumed to have characteristics that are more highly valued
- Little to no hierarchy
- Shows us potential for structures without domination
- Ex: Vanatinai Island, Papua New Guinea
	- Qualities of strength, wisdom, and generosity valued and assumed to be possible in everyone
	- Men have no formal authority over women
	- Similar access to political and economic roles
	- Similar access to the supernatural
	- Similar sexual freedom
	- Similar participation in reproductive labour
### Structures of Power
- Social structures: long lasting social arrangements that influence and limit what people do
	- Politics, economics, kinship, race, etc.
- Social structures are not 'neutral' --- rather, they are structures of power
- Socially constructed systems of classification (e.g. ways of categorizing) undergird social strucures
- Certain categories are more highly valued or more "normal" than others
- Varies based on cultural and historical context
- Ex. racial categories
- Institutions distribute resources to groups based on these classifications
- Material resources and symbolic resources (e.g. prestige, access to decision making, representation)
- It affects not only individuals, but how entire systems operate
	- You can have prestigious education as a black South African, but there would still be more privilege for white South Africans
### Privilege/Oppression
- Social structures produce privilege and oppression
- Often easier to recognize oppression, but they are two sides of the same coin
- Ex: Holding hands with partner in public
- Even if people don't consciously leverage their privilege, they indirectly benefit from it
	- Not condemnatory, but it is still important to acknowledge it
### Hegemony
- People are often unconsciously complicit in maintaining forms of privilege/oppression (even those in oppressed groups)
- The ability to of a dominant group to make its interests/values like "common sense"
- Hegemonic ideas benefit those in power
- Ex: CEOs should earn many times more than other workers
	- Can someone work hundreds more times than another person?
### Intersectionality
- Analaytic framework that talks about how structures of power cannot be separated from each other, but operates in tandem
- Kimberle Crenshaw - 1989
	- Case of Emma: Black and female, was discriminated with hiring
		- African americans hired were all men
		- Women hired were all white
		- Intesectionality between racism and sexism
- Many people are in positions of privilege in some ways, and oppression in others
- Can be both oppressed and oppressor simultaneously
- Must examine our own positionality to enact change
### Agency
- Oppression =/= passivity
- Potential of individuals to shape direction of their lives
- People can contest structures through overt actions or more subtle ones (protests, not completely conforming to norms)
- Structures and agency are in constant tension
