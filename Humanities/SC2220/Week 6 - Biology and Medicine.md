### Biomedicine
- "Western" or "mainstream" medicine
- Applies the principles of biology and natural sciences to diagnose diseases and to promote healing
- Contrasted with ethnomedicine---localized systems of health and healing rooted in culturally specific norms and values
### Ethnomedicine
- False dichotomy
- Biomedicine is a form of ethnomedicine
- Draws on European enlightenment values of rationality and individualism
- Individual body is the focus of treatment
- Reminded by Haraway: knowledge is not neutral
- Biomedicine is shaped by cultural norms regarding gender (and sexuality, race, etc.)
- "Sleeping metaphors"
- Despite contrary evidence, tendency to portray egg and sperm in stereotypical American gender roles
- Egg as "dominant bridge awaiting her mate's magic kiss, which instills the spirit that brings her to life"
### Biomedicine (contd.)
- Sex, gender, and sexuality are often thought of as biological "facts"
- Often thought of as binary categories
- Biomedicine plays a central role in producing these understandings
- Produces understandings of "healthy" and "normal" vs. "morbid" and "pathological"
- Practitioners have an outsized role in shaping ideas about gender and sexuality
	- Expert knowledge; society awards them authority
	- Patients own perceptions matter less
	- Practitioners may or may not be aware of their roles
- ICD-11 by the WHO
	- Used by more than 150 countries, translated into over 40 languages
- DSM-5 by the APA

### Classifications
- Tracing history shows us that classifications are social constructions rather than biological truths
- Influences the creation of "normalcy" and "pathology"
- Points towards the broader impacts of these categories as well as the importance of activism
### Homosexuality
- term first used in Europe and America in mid-1800s, specific to this context
- German psychiatrist Richard von Krafft-Ebing's 1886 *Psychopathia Sexualis*
- Influenced by Charles Darwin---Darwinian lens: non-procreative behaviors pathological
	- Homosexuality as a psychiatric disorder
- Linked to ideas about binary gender categories
	- Attraction to women as a masculine trait, attraction to men as a feminine trait
	- Gay individuals presumed to have brains that resembled those of opposite sex
- 1940s: Hungarian psychoanalyst Sandor Rado claims homosexuality as "phobic" avoidance of opposite sex due to bad parenting
- Practitioners offer "cures" for homosexuality
- 1952: DSM-1 lists homosexuality as "sociopathic personality disturbance"; later as a "sexual deviation"
- Different treatment based on class and gender
- In the mid-20th century UK, male homosexuality was criminalized
	- Working class men went to prison
	- Middle and upper class men were offered "aversion therapy"
	- Female sexuality not criminalized (but still seen as disorder)
	- No consensus about whether or not women could or should be "treated"
- Mid-20th century: sex researchers challenge these ideas
	- Alfred Kinsey: homosexuality is more common than previously believed
	- Evelyn Hooker: debunks myth that homosexualsare less mentally stable
	- APA largely ignores findings
- Gay activists argue psychiatric theories contribute to social stigma
	- Protests at 1970 and 1971 APA annual meetings
	- Deliberations within APA
	- Homosexuality removed from DSM in 1974
	- Removed from ICD in 1990
### Medical practitioners
- Practitioners try to overcome 'categorical crises' created by intersex and trans bodies
- "Healthy" bodies fit into binary categories
- Sees "successful" intersex and trans interventions as those that result stereotypical displays of gender that align with the patient's assigned sex and heterosexuality
	- e.g. HRT, surgery
- Sexed embodiments can challenge gender structure only if non-normative sexed embodiments are allowed rather than pathologized
