- Genesis 2:15
	- God settles man in the garden of Eden to  cultivate and care for it.
- Deuteronomy  5:13-15

- Deuteronomy  14:28-29

- Deuteronomy 24:14-15

##### Explanation
###### Pope Francis, Amoris Laetitia (The Joy of Love)
no. 23-24:
- Work is an essential part of human dignity; man is a laborer who works the earth, harnesses the forces of nature and produces "the bread of anxious toil".
	- Labor makes development of society possible. It provides for the sustenance, stability, and fruitfulness of one's family.
###### Pope Francis, Laudato Si (On Care for Our Common Home)
no. 127-28:
- Work should be the setting for rich personal growth:
	- Creativity, planning for the future, developing talents, living out values, relating to others
	- Giving glory to God
- In today's global society, we need prioritize the goal of access to steady employment to all.
	- We were created with the vocation to work.
	- Technology should NOT replace human work. Work is a necessity.
- Helping the poor financially is a provisional solution, but the broader objective is to give them dignity through work.
###### Pope Francis, Evangelii Gaudium (The Joy of the Gospel)
no. 204:
- For growth in justice, we need more than just economic growth:
	- It requires decisions, programs, and mechanisms for the better distribution of income
	- Creation of employment
	- Promotion for the poor beyond than just welfare.
	- NO to reducing workforce.
###### Pope Benedict XVI, Caritias in Veritate (Charity in Truth)
no. 25:
- The primary capital to be safeguarded and valued: man, the human person, in his or her integrity:
	- Man is the source, the focus, and the aim of all economic and social life. - Vatican II, Gaudium et Spes no. 36
 
no.36:
- The economic sphere is neither ethically neutral, or inherently inhuman or opposed to society.
- It is a part of human activity, and since it is human it must be structured and governed in an ethical manner.
- i.e. the structures are entirely based on our own volition.

no. 63:
- Poverty results from a violation of the dignity of human work, because:
	- Work opportunities are limited
	- Low value is put on work and its rights (especially the right to a just wage and the personal security of the worker and the family)

###### Laborem Exercens
no. 9:
- A person achieves their fundamental vocation to share in God's creative activity through work
- Akin to God's creation of the world, which was in itself work
- Through work, we are transformed ourselves to become more of a human being

no. 3:
- Human work is an essential key to the social question, of decisive importance when making life "human"

There is a subjective dimension of work (the person subject work and his vocation to work) holds primacy over the objective dimension (technical aspects of work)---this is the Christian criticism of economism and materialism where humans are reduced to instruments of production
- The primary basis of the value of work is man himself, who is its subject
- Work is not the person, not the person "for work"

no. 12:
- Labor has priority over capital, where all things are products of Earth's resources (God's free gift) transformed and made productive by human labor
- Capital is meant to serve labor and not the other way around.

no. 16-23:
- Three basic rights:
	- right to work, right to a just share in the fruits of production, right to organize "for the purpose of defending (workers') interests and contributing as responsible partners to the common good"
- Justice and equity as central values in determining fair wages for workers
- The State must contribute to the achievement of these goals directly and indirectly:
	- Subsidiarity, creating favorable conditions for the free exercise of economic activity
	- Solidarity, placing certain limits on the autonomy o the parties who determine working conditions and ensure that there is necessary minimum support for the unemployed

### Integrity of Creation
- Our relationship with the rest of creation:
	- We are not ultimate owners, but rather temporary stewards
	- We are entrusted with caring for the gits and preserving them for future generations
	- Material good and how we develop uses for them are God's gift; they bring out an image of God within us
- Man's dominion is not an absolute power:
	- There has always been a limitation, e.g. prohibition to "not eat the fruit of the tree"
	- We are subject to both biological and moral laws
- We are not God, the Earth was here before us and it has been given to us
	- As Christians, we are to reject the notion that we have absolute domination over other creatures
	- We are to till and keep, i.e. to cultivate while also preserving
	- We have the duty to protect the Earth's fruitfulness for the future
- It is unjust that a privileged few accumulate excess resources while the masses are in misery
	- The ecological breakdown shows the extent of greed and selfishness' contrary to the order of creation
- Responsibility for God's Earth means that human beings must respect laws of nature
	- The Bible has no place for tyrannical anthropocentrism unconcerned for other creatures
- All living beings have a value in God's eyes
	- They are not to be completely subordinated for the good of humans
- Other living beings are not objects subjected to human domination
	- If nature is viewed as source of profit, it has consequences for society
	- We are to reject all tyrannical domination
- Other creatures are companions, fellow creatures --- their existence is utterly dependent on God's act of creativity and sustenance
