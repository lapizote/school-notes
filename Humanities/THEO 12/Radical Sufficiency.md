A Lodestar and Ethic for Facing the Challenges of our Time

Speaker: Dr. Christine Firer Hinze

### Radical Sufficiency
- Economy's purpose: to ensure "enough for all" -- a dignified livelihood for every person, household, and family
- Economic justice: sustainable sufficiency, security, status for all workers and families
- The personal communal, and policy mindsets and practices needed to advance a sustainable, inclusive-livelihood economic future
- Radical -> Meaning what?
- Sufficiency -> Meaning what?
- In a radical sufficiency economic and social order, everyone can participate and everyone has access to enough
### Part I: CST'S Wide-Angle Lens & Key Principles
- Buoys in a stormy sea: CST's Navigational Guides for a post-pandemic economy and society
- CST's Key Principles: Gospel-based "navigational guides"
##### I. Background
- 19th Century Catholic Church seeks to address 'the social question':
	- Impacts of modern industrialization on workers
- Today: CST Continues to champion economic justice or vulnerable persons, workers and families
- CST's Foundational Premise: The inviolable, god-given dignity and worth of every person
	- This dignity is personal and relational
- The common good:
	- Human dignity is realized by participation in multiple, related communities
- Social and Structural Sin:
	- We live in a broken world. Sin and evil are real.
	- Structures of sin: Personal sins accumulate into "common bads"
	- With harmful cultural, institutional, systemic, and intergenerational effects
- Pope John Paul II: Practicing solidarity = serving the common good = antidote to structural sin
- Practice of solidarity: Combat structural sin (common 'bads') and help foster common goods
- CST and Liberation Theology: Solidarity requires a preferential concern for the poor, vulnerable, and marginalized
- Including an "option for" our vulnerable earth, solidarity and sustainability go hand-in-hand
- CST's Preferential Solidarity has demanding personal, social, and policy implications:
	- The needs of the poor have priority over the rich.
	- The rights of workers are more important than the maximization of profits.
	- The participation of marginalized groups has precedence over a system which excludes them.
	- Radical!
### Part II: CST Economy for All
- Economy's Historical Meaning: Household Management
- Oikonomia's Aim: Ample sufficiency for all in the household -> "enough"
- Modern Economy's Focus: Maximizing Profit, Wealth, Growth.
- CST's Focus: Economy's classical meaning and purpose 'inclusive provisioning'
- What is Economy's Purpose?
	- To produce and make accessible ample sufficiency --- 'enough' for all members
	- via: Dignified participation and work and a dignified livelihood (sufficiency, security, status) for all
	- Work is fundamental to the dignity of the person.
- 20th Century: Msgr. John A. Ryan sums up CST:
	- In work and economic activity, we purpose and have the right to fulfill basic human needs and drives for sufficiency, security, and status.
- CST Today: A fourth, urgent "s" -> sustainability
	- Genuine care for our own lives and our relationships - Pope Francis
- Versus modern, dominant consumerist/market culture.
	- You always need more!
	- Consumerism makes a religion of consuming.
- Mass consumerist culture cultivates desires (needs?) for:
	- More! Better! Newer! Most up-to-date! Keeping up with my peers!
- "Enough" is an ever-receding goal; wanting new/better/more, excess are normalized; waste is rampant.
- Pope Francis: Consumerism fosters a 'throw-away culture' where things, labor, resources, and people are commodified and disposable.
- Take notice! Consumerist ideology and culture constantly stimulates and entices our deep needs for 'sufficiency, security, and status' and our anxieties about those needs.
### Part III: From CST 'Talk' to 'Transformative Walk'
- How do we change, and become agents of change?
- For people and systems formed by market consumerist orthodoxy, oving towards a radical sufficiency ecnomy will require potentially costly change
- "Clenched", closed-in fearful minds and hearts cling to security and resist change.
	- We are scared to be vulnerable.
- To 'walk CST talk' of solidarity and radical sufficiency, we need root level reorientation. We need conversion.
- "Conversion", writes Jesuite theologian Bernard Lonergan, is "a shift of one's fundamental orientation... from self-enclosure to self-transcendence."
- Conversation alters the horizons of one's knowing and caring: intellectual, moral, affective or religious
- Conversion is a process of opening hearts, minds, hands; some theologians compare it to falling --and dwelling-- in love
- Conversion is a multifacted, continually-renewing process:
	- Conversion to truth, to reality
	- Drive to discover and honor what is real and true (Intellectual!)
	- Conversion to value
	- Drive to discover and follow what is good (Moral!)
	- COnversion to love and to love of God
	- Drive to freely give oneself in service to neighbor and to the common good (Heart/Religious!)
- Most deeply-cultivating solidarity and sufficiency is a path of spiritual conversion - in god we find our true sufficiency, security, status
	- "The lord is my shepherd I SHALL NOT WANT..."
- But how?
	- How---personally, in our families, school, wider communities - can we do our parts to promote CST
- Changing and working for change are ongoing journeys. The road is often bumpy, circuitous uncertain and messy.
	- Risk.
- Vs. 'Yoyo' --- we need: people and communities dedicated to ongoing conversion and common action.
- The challenging path to solidarity and sufficiency 'is a journey best taken together'. Above all, do not let yourselves be robbed of hope! - Pope Francis
